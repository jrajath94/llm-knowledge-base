How to Use This Curriculum

This is the operating manual for the whole program. Read it first, once, then start Volume 1. It tells you the order to study, how to pace 4+ hours a day, how the volumes connect, and how the capstone project threads through everything.

1. What this curriculum promises

When you finish, you can do three things:

  1. Explain how an LLM is built end to end. Data, tokenization, pre-training, distributed training, post-training and RL, evaluation, inference and serving. No hand-waving. You will have built a tiny one yourself.
  2. Think like a researcher. Read a paper and extract its real claim. Design an experiment with baselines and ablations. Know what a result proves and what it does not.
  3. Ship like an engineer. Take a model from a notebook to production: pipelines, monitoring, cost control, reliability. Build solutions that work on the planet, not just in a demo.

That combination, research depth plus production proof, is what L5-L7 Research Engineer, Applied AI Engineer, and ML Engineer loops at frontier labs actually test.

3. Pacing: 4+ hours a day

Total is roughly 9-10 weeks at 4+ hours a day, with the DSA track running 1-2 hours a day in parallel. A day that works:

Block What to do
Hour 1-2 Read one chapter deeply. Work the examples by hand. Trace the numbers.
Hour 3 Do the lab. Run the code. Break one thing on purpose and observe what changes.
Hour 4 DSA track: one pattern, 2-3 problems. Or paper reading in Phase 4.
Bonus time Re-explain the day's hardest idea out loud, from memory, in plain words. If you cannot, you do not know it yet.

The re-explanation rule. Reading is not learning. Every day ends with you explaining the hardest idea of the day out loud, from memory, in simple sentences. This is the single highest-leverage habit in the program.

Attention management. Work in 50-minute blocks with 10-minute breaks. One volume at a time; do not parallel-read volumes. If a chapter feels easy, do its lab anyway, the lab is where the understanding actually forms.

4. The capstone: build an LLM from scratch

One project threads through Volumes 1-8. You build the same tiny model up, volume by volume:

Volume Capstone lab
1 · Math Autograd from scratch in NumPy: differentiate a tiny network by hand first, then verify with code
4 · LLM internals Attention in NumPy/JAX with worked numerics; a BPE tokenizer trained from scratch
5 · Pre-training Train a tiny GPT on a toy corpus; plot loss curves; feel what learning-rate and batch-size knobs do
6 · Distributed Data-parallel toy run; apply FSDP concepts to the tiny model; profile where time goes
7 · Post-training SFT then DPO on the tiny model; measure the behavior shift with evals you write
8 · Inference Serve the tiny model with a KV cache; benchmark latency and throughput; try quantization

By Volume 8 you have personally touched every stage of the LLM lifecycle. In an interview, "I built a tiny model end to end" plus the ability to explain each stage is worth more than any certificate.

5. How to study each volume

Every volume follows the same anatomy. Learn it once:

  • Chapters teach one idea each, from zero. Every chapter has: what it is, why it exists, how it works under the hood, a worked example with real numbers, a common misunderstanding (the interview trap), a visual with a walkthrough, and an interview-relevance line.
  • "How to read this diagram" walkthroughs sit under every significant figure. Read them. They are the fastest path to the mental model.
  • Labs are runnable code. Run them. Change one variable at a time and observe. Mark GPU labs "RunPod-ready" when a GPU helps.
  • "On the board" boxes list the interview questions each chapter answers. Cover the answers and quiz yourself before moving on.

What "done" means for a chapter. You can (a) define every term in plain words, (b) work the example's numbers yourself, (c) explain the diagram to someone else, (d) answer its "On the board" questions out loud. If any of the four fails, re-read before advancing.

6. The parallel DSA track

Data structures and algorithms run alongside the spine, not inside it: 1-2 hours a day, pattern by pattern. The track file is the Top-300 LeetCode Mastery Path: organized by pattern, with code templates and deep links into the existing 500-problem atlas.

Rule: patterns before problems. Learn the pattern's template, then solve 2-3 problems with it, then move on. Never grind problems without naming the pattern first.

8. Ground rules

  • No skipping the math. Volume 1 is the foundation. Everything later stands on it.
  • Labs are mandatory. Reading about training is not training. Run the code.
  • One volume at a time. Finish the volume, do its capstone lab, then advance.
  • Honest markers. Where a volume says a number is illustrative or a treatment is reconstructed from first principles, believe it. The curriculum never invents facts.
  • Generic and portable. These files contain no personal information and work fully offline. Copy them anywhere.
