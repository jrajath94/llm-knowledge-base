Unit1

Unit 1 · The map

How to read this atlas

From the lesson 16-ai-lab-atlas.html, opening section

The AI Lab is a 15-week, 73-lab sequence. This atlas is the map of the whole sequence. Each week chapter lists every lab day, links to the full walkthrough on its category page, and shows the difficulty and the task count at a glance.

Weeks map to the category volumes. The 11 categories map to volumes 17 through 27, and each volume holds the full task-by-task walkthrough of its weeks. Weeks 1 and 2 open on Python data tools and math, volumes 17 and 18. Weeks 3 and 4 cover classical models, volume 19. The pattern continues through agents in week 15, volume 27.

Weeks also pair with the theory spine. Volumes 1 through 15 teach the theory, and the lab weeks teach the practice. The lab week never repeats the theory chapter. It assumes the reader read the chapter first.

Follow the weeks in order. Each week builds on the last. Week 3 assumes the probability from week 2. Week 6 assumes the evaluation habits of week 5. Week 11 assumes the transformers of weeks 9 and 10.

On each category page, the tab bar jumps to any lab. Every lab opens with a framing paragraph, and the page keeps each task collapsed until the reader opens it. A week holds 3 to 6 lab days. The second chart below shows the study plan for the atlas itself.

Unit2

Unit 2 · The ramp

Difficulty progression

From the lesson 16-ai-lab-atlas.html, difficulty section

The progression is a staircase with four landings. Weeks 1 and 2 are Easy fundamentals: data tools and the math the models need. Weeks 3 through 6 are Medium: classical models, evaluation habits, and the first neural networks. Weeks 7 through 11 are Hard: the full deep learning and transformer block. Weeks 12 through 15 are Expert: production systems, system design, and agents.

Phase 1, weeks 1 to 2, holds 8 Easy labs. Phase 2, weeks 3 to 6, holds 21 Medium labs. Phase 3, weeks 7 to 11, holds 26 Hard labs. Phase 4, weeks 12 to 15, holds 18 Expert labs. The chart below counts the labs at each level.

The expert block at the end has the fewest labs per week. Each lab is a design problem rather than a coding exercise, so the effort stays high.

26Hard labs, the largest difficulty block
Nugget 2 · The staircase From the lesson Difficulty progression
The lesson

The difficulty counts are 8 Easy, 21 Medium, 26 Hard, and 18 Expert labs, in four phases.

Why it matters. The steepest step is week 7, where Medium becomes Hard at the moment real network training starts.

Transfer. Budget energy by phase, not by week. The Hard block is the long climb.

Unit3

Unit 3 · Tools first

Week 1: Python data tools

From the lesson 16-ai-lab-atlas.html, week 1

Three Easy labs build the working surface for all later work. The NumPy Lab holds 15 tasks on day 2. The Pandas Lab holds 8 tasks on day 3. The EDA Lab + Homework holds 8 tasks on day 4.

Exploratory data analysis, or EDA, means a load of a dataset and a probe of the dataset with plots and summary statistics before model work. Every later lab assumes fluency here: load a dataset, slice it, and check shapes without pause.

NumPy, pandas, and EDA are the assumed background for all 70 later labs. The week goal is fluency, not new concepts.

Unit4

Unit 4 · The math in use

Week 2: The math you will actually use

From the lesson 16-ai-lab-atlas.html, week 2

Five Easy labs cover the math the models need. Day 1 is Linear Algebra for ML, 6 tasks. Day 2 is Calculus & Gradients, 5 tasks. Day 3 is Probability Essentials, 6 tasks. Day 4 is Statistics for ML, 6 tasks. Day 5 is Math Lab (Linear Algebra + Probability), 6 tasks.

A gradient is the direction of steepest change. Training uses gradients to improve the model. Gradients and probability pay off at once in week 3.

The final math lab mixes linear algebra with probability. The source notes that most interview questions combine that exact pair. Pair this week with Volume 1 math and Volume 2 ML foundations.

Unit5

Unit 5 · First models

Week 3: Classical models I

From the lesson 16-ai-lab-atlas.html, week 3

The first real models arrive at Medium difficulty. Day 1 is Logistic Regression Gradient & Convexity, 8 tasks. Day 2 is Linear Regression & GLMs, 8 tasks. Day 3 is Logistic Regression & Calibration, 6 tasks. Day 4 is kNN & Distance-Based Learning, 7 tasks. Day 5 is Probabilistic ML Thinking (Naive Bayes), 7 tasks.

Logistic regression teaches gradient-based fitting and convexity. Gradient-based fitting means a change of parameters by a move along gradients downhill. Linear regression introduces generalized linear models, or GLMs.

Calibration matters in this week: the probabilities a model predicts must match reality. The source calls these five models the baselines that interviewers compare all later work against.

Unit6

Unit 6 · The zoo completes

Week 4: Classical models II

From the lesson 16-ai-lab-atlas.html, week 4

Day 1 is Decision Trees, 8 tasks. Day 2 is Support Vector Machines (Interview Level), 5 tasks. Day 3 is Ensemble Methods & Boosting, 6 tasks. Day 4 is Classical ML Failure Modes, 6 tasks. Day 5 is Classical ML Lab, 7 tasks.

Decision trees, support vector machines, and ensemble methods finish the classical model set. Trees plus ensembles are the workhorse models for tabular data.

The failure modes day is the most interview-relevant day of the classical block. Failure modes are the ways these models break in practice. After this week the reader holds a complete classical toolkit before the deep block.

Unit7

Unit 7 · Tell good from bad

Week 5: Evaluation and debugging

From the lesson 16-ai-lab-atlas.html, week 5

This week separates people who train models from people who can tell whether a model is good. Feature engineering comes first. A study of evaluation in depth comes next. Then the bias-variance tradeoff with regularization: simple models underfit, and complex models overfit. Then systematic debugging and error analysis. The week ends with a PCA lab, principal component analysis.

The source states the stakes plainly. Evaluation and debugging questions appear in almost every ML loop.

The atlas names week 5 as one of the three weeks to protect under time pressure, with weeks 7 and 10.

Unit8

Unit 8 · The bridge

Week 6: Neural networks I

From the lesson 16-ai-lab-atlas.html, week 6

The bridge week opens with data preprocessing pipelines and data engineering, the plain half of deep learning work. Then the week builds the first neural network and closes with a backpropagation lab.

Backpropagation is the algorithm that computes gradients through the layers of a network, so train runs are possible. This is the last Medium week. It is the on-ramp to the Hard block.

Unit9

Unit 9 · The steep step

Week 7: Neural networks II

From the lesson 16-ai-lab-atlas.html, week 7

Difficulty steps to Hard, and networks stop as black boxes. Activation functions and initialization explain how signals survive through layers. Optimization methods explain how a train run converges. Day 3 trains a small network end to end. The week closes with CNN Basics, 4 tasks, and Modern CNN Architectures (Residual Networks), 4 tasks.

The Medium-to-Hard step here is the steepest in the curriculum. The source adds a plain rule: a train run on one small network by hand teaches more than a read about ten networks.

Unit10

Unit 10 · Two modalities

Week 8: CNNs in practice, then text

From the lesson 16-ai-lab-atlas.html, week 8

Day 1 is Transfer Learning & Fine-Tuning, 7 tasks. Day 2 is Augmentation Strategies, 8 tasks. Day 3 is CNN Lab , Image Classification (Frozen Backbone Workflow), 6 tasks. Day 4 is Text Preprocessing & Tokenization, 5 tasks. Day 5 is Classical NLP Methods, 9 tasks.

Three days make CNNs work in practice: transfer learning, augmentation, and a full image-classification lab on a frozen backbone. Then the week pivots to text. The pivot is deliberate: week 9 starts sequence models, and sequence models need text as numbers first.

Transfer learning is the practical answer to a small dataset. Tokenization choices in this week follow the reader into weeks 9 through 11.

Unit11

Unit 11 · Sequences to attention

Week 9: From sequences to attention

From the lesson 16-ai-lab-atlas.html, week 9

Day 1 is Embeddings & Representation, 11 tasks. Day 2 is RNNs & LSTMs (Brief), 6 tasks. Day 3 is NLP Lab , Text Classification, 9 tasks. Day 4 is ML Coding , Vectorized Softmax, 6 tasks. Day 5 is Self-Attention & Scaled Dot-Product, 7 tasks.

Embeddings give words and items vector forms a model can learn. RNNs and LSTMs show the older way to model sequences, as history. Self-attention on day 5 lets a model weigh which other positions matter for each position.

Self-attention on day 5 is the seed of all work in weeks 10 and 11. Embeddings return as the core of retrieval in week 11.

Unit12

Unit 12 · The peak

Week 10: Transformers

From the lesson 16-ai-lab-atlas.html, week 10

Day 1 is Transformer Architecture, 10 tasks. Day 2 is LLM Training Dynamics, 10 tasks. Day 3 holds two labs: Tokenization Deep Dive, 6 tasks, and Transformer Lab , Tiny Autoregressive LM (Offline), 9 tasks. Day 4 is LLM Lab , Fine-Tuning, 6 tasks. Day 5 is Attention Complexity Optimizations.

An autoregressive language model generates text one token at a time, with each token conditioned on the tokens before it. By Friday of this week the student has built and trained one.

Six labs and 47 tasks make this the peak week of the sequence. A fine-tuning lab and attention complexity work complete the week.

47tasks in week 10, the peak week

Unit13

Unit 13 · Retrieval

Week 11: Retrieval and RAG

From the lesson 16-ai-lab-atlas.html, week 11

Retrieval-augmented generation, or RAG, gives a language model access to external documents at query time, in place of trust in the weights alone. The week builds the full stack: embeddings for retrieval, a vector index, and a generation step over the retrieved text.

The week assumes the transformers of weeks 9 and 10. Embeddings from week 9 return here as the core of retrieval.

Unit14

Unit 14 · Production

Week 12: MLOps and production systems

From the lesson 16-ai-lab-atlas.html, week 12

The first Expert week changes the focus: from one good model to many models with reliable runs. MLOps, machine learning operations, covers pipelines and the production habits around a model: deploy steps, monitor steps, and retrain steps.

The lab work here is design work. Each lab poses a production problem rather than a coding exercise.

Unit15

Unit 15 · Design one

Week 13: System design I

From the lesson 16-ai-lab-atlas.html, week 13

Design interviews begin here. The week covers the fundamentals of ML system design, feature store patterns, real-time versus batch inference, and case studies of recommendation, search, and ads systems.

A design round has no single right answer. The lab trains skill at tradeoffs at scale and at a clear defense of each choice.

Unit16

Unit 16 · Design two

Week 14: System design II

From the lesson 16-ai-lab-atlas.html, week 14

Harder designs follow. A full real-time recommendation system. LLM inference system design. A study of feature stores and data pipelines in depth. Distributed training at scale.

This week pairs directly with distributed training and inference-serving interview rounds. The scaling-training lab is the bridge to the GPU and systems volumes.

Unit17

Unit 17 · The capstone

Week 15: LLM and agentic AI

From the lesson 16-ai-lab-atlas.html, week 15

The capstone week holds LLMs in production, an agents introduction, multi-agent systems in production, inference-time scaling with verifiers, and self-improving agents.

An agent is a system that uses a language model to plan and take actions, not just generate text. All fourteen prior weeks converge here. The week reuses transformers from week 10, RAG from week 11, serving from week 12, and design from weeks 13 and 14.

4steps in the lab study loop
Nugget 4 · The study loop From the lesson How to read this atlas
The lesson

Each lab runs one loop: read the task, try the starter code, open hints only when stuck, open the solution only after an answer or a real failure.

Why it matters. Collapsed solutions are the study discipline of the whole lab program. A solution read early converts a lab into a summary.

Transfer. Hold the loop on every lab. The loop, not the lab count, produces the skill.

Unit 18 · The rounds

Interview clearance

From the lesson 16-ai-lab-atlas.html, closing section

Each week block clears specific interview rounds. Volume 28 works the full question bank. This closing table tells the reader which weeks to study for which round.

Weeks 1 and 2 clear the data and math screens. The classical and evaluation weeks clear the ML fundamentals rounds. The transformer and retrieval weeks clear the LLM rounds. Week 15 clears the post-training deep dive and the inference and serving rounds, with agents in production, inference-time scaling, and verifiers. All 15 weeks together form the full preparation.
